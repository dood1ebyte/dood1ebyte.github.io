Where EU public tenders get only one bid

Single-bid shares by country and sector, from the most recent TED award notices (up to 12 months to October 2026).

A lot that attracts a single bid is one where the winner faced no competition, and where a newcomer would have faced one rival at most. Across the EU, the share of such lots varies widely by country and sector. We computed it for 27 EU countries and 10 sectors from award notices on TED, the EU's official procurement journal.

Key findings

  • The least contested markets (lots with a reported number of bids, at least 100 per cell):
    • Repair and maintenance in Croatia: 87% of lots received a single bid (median 1 bid; 1014 lots).
    • Repair and maintenance in Poland: 79% of lots received a single bid (median 1 bid; 170 lots).
    • IT services in Croatia: 79% of lots received a single bid (median 1 bid; 637 lots).
    • Software in Greece: 77% of lots received a single bid (median 1 bid; 614 lots).
    • Software in Croatia: 76% of lots received a single bid (median 1 bid; 364 lots).
    • IT services in Slovenia: 73% of lots received a single bid (median 1 bid; 413 lots).
  • The most contested:
    • Engineering in Finland: 4% single-bid (median 6 bids; 482 lots).
    • Construction in Estonia: 7% single-bid (median 5 bids; 243 lots).
    • Construction in Denmark: 8% single-bid (median 4 bids; 726 lots).
    • Construction in Finland: 8% single-bid (median 5 bids; 361 lots).
    • Engineering in Estonia: 9% single-bid (median 6 bids; 375 lots).
  • By sector, the median country single-bid share is Repair 46%; Software 45%; Medical 44%; Transport 38%; IT services 36%; Office/IT hardware 28%; Cleaning/env. 28%; Business services 23%; Engineering 22%; Construction 16%.
  • By country (median across sectors with enough lots): highest Greece 61%, Slovenia 50%, Poland 50%, Bulgaria 47%, Czechia 44%; lowest Netherlands 24%, Ireland 18%, Finland 18%, France 17%, Denmark 14%.

Method

  • Source: TED Search API v3 (open data), result notices published from 2025-10-31 to 2026-10-31, by buyer country and CPV division (30 Office/IT hardware, 33 Medical, 34 Transport, 45 Construction, 48 Software, 50 Repair, 71 Engineering, 72 IT services, 79 Business services, 90 Cleaning/env.).
  • Sample: the newest 500 result notices per country and sector. In 131 of the 270 country-sector pairs, more than 500 notices were published in the 12 months, so their sample covers only the most recent part of the year. The tables count the lots in those notices that report a number of tenders received.
  • Single-bid share: lots that received exactly one tender, divided by lots that received at least one. Lots that received none are excluded from it.
  • Review (2026-10-31): every figure in the findings and both tables was recomputed from the raw output, with 0 mismatches.
  • Code: research/procurement/competition_report.mjs, which uses the same statistics as EU Tender Radar (competitionStats). Raw output: docs/growth/data/ted-competition-2026-10-31.md (CI run 41149991525).

Limits

  • TED publishes procurement above the EU thresholds only. Smaller national tenders are not included.
  • Countries report lot results differently, and some notices carry no bid counts. The share describes the lots that report one. In Poland and Lithuania, fewer than half of the sampled notices yield a lot with a bid count (median 0.43 and 0.37 lots per notice), so their figures rest on a minority of notices.
  • Where a country published more than 500 result notices in a sector, the sample covers its most recent months, not the whole year.
  • Framework agreements and reserved or negotiated procedures are counted like any other lot.
